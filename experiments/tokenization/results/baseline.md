# E3 baseline: token cost of the AI message corpus

This summary was generated by `baseline.py`. Every number was measured by this script, and the raw data is in `baseline.json`. The corpus is `corpus/ai_messages.json` (40 messages).

## 0. Summary

1. **en baseline**: the 40 messages total 1159 (llama4) to 1268 (mistral_sp) tokens; on o200k, mean 29.0, median 27.0, p90 41.0 tokens per message.
2. **en_terse (terse English, the strongest competitor without a spec)**: total ratio vs en 0.73 (claude_legacy) to 0.76 (mistral_sp), 7-tokenizer mean 0.74. o200k 0.73, claude_legacy 0.73. Share of messages with fewer tokens than en (o200k) 100%.
3. **ko (Korean)**: total ratio vs en 1.16 (llama4) to 2.29 (mistral_sp), 7-tokenizer mean 1.65. o200k 1.30, claude_legacy 2.21. Share of messages with fewer tokens than en (o200k) 0%.
4. **json (compact JSON)**: total ratio vs en 0.94 (cl100k) to 1.13 (mistral_sp), 7-tokenizer mean 1.00. o200k 0.97, claude_legacy 1.06. Share of messages with fewer tokens than en (o200k) 52%.
5. **oracle_min** (per message, the minimum of en / en_terse / json): ratio vs en 0.73–0.76, mean 0.73. Number of times each variant gave the minimum (280 cells of message × tokenizer; ties all counted): en 0, en_terse 271, json 13.
6. **14 messages with escape targets (numbers, paths, identifiers, URLs, proper nouns) vs 26 without** (ratio vs en, 7-tokenizer mean): en_terse 0.77 vs 0.72, json 1.03 vs 0.98. Mean en tokens per message on o200k 32.2 vs 27.3.
7. **Target line for this language**: to beat en_terse, which is readable without a spec, the mean tokens per message must be below o200k 21.2, claude_legacy 21.4 (en is 29.0 / 29.5). Converted with the E2 tokens/morpheme for W2_spaced + token_picked, that means fewer than o200k 21.0, claude_legacy 19.0 morphemes per message (section 7). The cost of putting the spec (thousands of tokens) in the context is not included in this comparison.

Writing guidelines

- The four variants carry the same information. Small numbers are written as words (five) in en and as digits in en_terse and json.
- en_terse drops articles and copulas and uses symbols such as `->`, `=`, `+`, `~`, `;`. Abbreviations were kept only when measuring them with the tokenizers showed they did not add tokens (for example, `w/`, `Ctx`, `2nd`, `O(n^2)` take more tokens than `with`, `Context`, `second`, `quadratic`, so they were not used).
- json uses short keys and serialization without whitespace (`separators=(',', ':')`), and most values are snake_case. Proper nouns and URLs are kept as in the original.
- ko is natural Korean. Foreign names of people and places are transliterated into Hangul; paths, identifiers and URLs are kept as in the original.
- The only real service URL is the Python documentation. The other names (people, the repository example-org/tilemap) are fictional.

## 8. Limitations

- The corpus is 40 messages written by one model in one pass. The style may be skewed, and the sample is small, so the second decimal place of the ratios is not reliable.
- The author aligned the four variants to carry the same information, but no person or other model verified this. Whether en_terse is understood correctly without a spec could not be checked without a model API, so it was not measured.
- The current Claude tokenizer is not public. claude_legacy is a proxy.
- The abbreviations in en_terse were chosen by measuring with these tokenizers, so they are biased in the competitor's favor (on purpose).
- json is a fairly compact form with short keys and snake_case values. Real JSON between agents may cost more because of longer keys or whitespace.
- Escape detection is a heuristic. All-caps abbreviations are not counted.
- Extra message tokens such as chat templates and role markers, and the cost of the spec (context), were not counted.
